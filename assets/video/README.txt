HERO VIDEO
==========

The home page hero plays assets/video/hero.mp4 if that file exists.
If it does not, the browser falls through to software.webm, which is a screen
recording of our own mockups produced by `npm run video`.

To use real footage, put the file here and name it exactly:

    assets/video/hero.mp4

Then run once:

    npm run hero

That switches the home page over to it. Delete the file and run it again to go
back to the software recording. Nothing else to edit.


WHAT TO SHOOT
-------------
Length        12 to 25 seconds. It loops, so end near where it started.
Resolution    1920 x 1080 is plenty. 4K is wasted at this size.
Frame         Subject on the RIGHT half. The headline sits on the left and has
              to stay readable over whatever is behind it.
Motion        Slow. A locked off shot with small movement inside the frame beats
              a moving camera every time.
Audio         None. The video is muted and can never be unmuted.
Lighting      Window light. Overhead fluorescent goes green on phone cameras.
File size     Under about 6 MB. The software recording is 2.6 MB for comparison.
              If a clip is much bigger, shorten it rather than lowering quality.
Format        H.264 MP4. Every phone shoots this. No conversion needed.

Do not burn captions, logos or text into the video. The page supplies those.


A SHOT LIST YOU CAN RUN IN TEN MINUTES
--------------------------------------
Phone on a tripod, or propped on a stack of books. Landscape. 4K or 1080p, 30fps.
Shoot each one for 20 seconds and keep the best 15.

1. Over the shoulder at a monitor with one of the mockups open, hand moving to
   the mouse, screen switching between two of the three tabs.
2. Two people at a table looking at one laptop, shot from behind and to the
   side, faces not readable. Reads as collaboration without being a stock photo.
3. Hands on a keyboard, shallow angle, screen glow, nothing else in focus.
4. A whiteboard with a real process sketched on it, hand adding one box.
5. The wide room: desk, monitors, window light, nobody in frame.

Any one of those beats stock footage. Number 1 is the strongest for this site,
because the thing on the screen is the thing you sell.


THREE WAYS TO GET IT
--------------------
1. Shoot it yourself, using the list above. Free, real, ten minutes.
2. Licensed stock. Pexels and Coverr are free and allow commercial use.
   Artgrid and Storyblocks are paid and far less recognisable.
3. Two hours of a local videographer. Roughly the cost of one project, and the
   footage gets used for years.


A NOTE ON STOCK FOOTAGE OF PEOPLE
---------------------------------
Buyers recognise stock. A studio site showing a stock team implies a team that
may not exist, which is the one claim this site is careful never to make.
Hands, desks, screens and a room read as real without asserting a headcount.
That is why the shot list above avoids faces.
